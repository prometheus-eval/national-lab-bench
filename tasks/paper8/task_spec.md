# Task: Develop a Substantive Follow-up to "Parameterized Physics-informed Neural Networks for Parameterized PDEs"

## Objective — Here's what you're doing

Develop a substantive scientific follow-up to P²INN by Cho, Jo, Lim, Lee, Lee, Hong, and Park, "Parameterized Physics-informed Neural Networks for Parameterized PDEs," ICML 2024. P²INN solves a family of parameterized PDEs with a single network by **encoding the PDE coefficients into a latent representation** and decoding the solution from it, rather than treating the coefficients as extra input coordinates. But it conditions on the **PDE coefficients only**: the initial and boundary conditions are imposed through the loss, never represented as inputs to the parameter encoder — so a single trained model cannot be *queried* at a new initial condition or a new boundary condition, only at a new coefficient. Your contribution is to **extend the parameter encoder into a problem-specification encoder** that conditions jointly on the coefficients **and** the initial condition **and** the boundary condition, so that one model generalizes across all three — and, at the outer edge, across PDE types — and to establish, with a mechanism analysis, that a single shared model genuinely represents this broader family rather than merely memorizing the trained specifications.

This is not a "reach a target error on a fixed construct" task. The construct itself is what you broaden: the object of study is *what a single parameterized-PINN surrogate can be conditioned on and generalize over*, and the evidence you must produce is that the extended encoding represents the initial/boundary conditions in a way that generalizes to unseen ones — not that a number dropped on the same coefficient-only benchmark.

## The Problem — Here's why it matters

Parameterized PDEs must be solved at many points in a **problem-specification space** — not only many PDE coefficients, but many initial conditions (different sources, different starting profiles) and many boundary conditions (periodic, Dirichlet, Neumann, mixed). In design optimization, uncertainty quantification, and inverse problems the initial and boundary conditions vary as much as the coefficients do. Training one PINN per specification is prohibitive. P²INN amortizes across **coefficients**: a **parameter encoder** `g_{θp}` maps the coefficient vector `μ` to a latent `h_param`, a **coordinate encoder** `g_{θc}` maps `(x,t)` to `h_coord`, and a **decoder** `g_{θg}` maps the concatenation to the solution — one model covers a whole coefficient range and overcomes PINN "failure modes" (high convection, high reaction) that defeat a per-instance PINN.

But the initial condition and the boundary condition enter P²INN **only through the training loss** — the IC/data term and the periodic-boundary term — and are **fixed** for a given trained model. Nothing about the initial or boundary condition is an *input* to the encoder, so the model cannot be evaluated at a new IC or a new BC without retraining. This is the method's central representational limitation: it conditions on one axis of the specification (coefficients) and bakes in the other two. **The open question this follow-up addresses: can the parameter encoder be extended into a problem-specification encoder that conditions on the initial and boundary conditions as inputs — so that one model generalizes to unseen initial conditions, unseen boundary conditions, unseen coefficient/IC/BC combinations, and across PDE types — and can we show, mechanistically, that the extended encoding actually represents the specification rather than ignoring it or memorizing the trained set?** Whether initial and boundary conditions can be encoded in a form that generalizes (defining parameters, basis coefficients, sampled function values, or a learned function encoder), whether a single shared model can span more than one PDE family, and whether the generalization comes from the *structured* conditioning rather than from merely adding raw inputs, is exactly what a rigorous follow-up must establish.

## Background — Here's what you need to know about the original paper

Read `paper.md` in full before beginning. The components you will **hold fixed** and build on:

1. **The three-part factorization.** `u_Θ(x,t;μ) = g_{θg}([ g_{θc}(x,t) ; g_{θp}(μ) ])`: a **coordinate encoder** `g_{θc}` producing `h_coord`, a **parameter encoder** `g_{θp}` producing `h_param = g_{θp}(μ)`, and a **decoder** `g_{θg}` that consumes their **concatenation** and outputs the solution. The original P²INN coordinate encoder, decoder, and concatenation-based injection of `h_param` and `h_coord` must remain unchanged, including the reference `modvec` pathway, as specified under Held fixed. Architectural changes are restricted to the specification encoder. The three sub-networks are trained **jointly, end-to-end**, under one loss.

2. **The parameter encoder `g_{θp}` (the object you extend).** A small MLP (the `param_enc_layer_1..4` stack, `3 → 150 → 150 → 150 → 50`, `tanh` between layers, in `code/P2INN/model.py`) that maps the PDE coefficient vector `μ` (for the CDR family `μ = (β, ν, ρ)`) to `h_param ∈ ℝ^{50}`. **In the reference model its input is the 3-dimensional coefficient vector and nothing else.** Extending *what this encoder conditions on* — turning it from a coefficient encoder into a problem-specification encoder that also ingests the initial condition and the boundary condition — is the contribution.

3. **The training loss and how ICs/BCs enter it.** A weighted sum (equal weights, `=1` in code) of an **IC data-matching term** `cost_gt` (predicted vs. ground-truth solution at the `t=0` initial-condition points), the **PDE-residual terms** `cost_f` (at interior collocation points) and `cost_u` (the same residual via `Loss_f.PDE_cal` additionally enforced at the IC points), and a **periodic-boundary term** `cost_bd`. **The initial condition sets the profile the `cost_gt` data-term matches; the boundary condition sets the boundary term** — both are properties of the *loss*, fixed per trained model, never inputs to `g_{θp}`. Training is self-supervised on the PDE residual and conditions; a ground-truth solution is needed only to *evaluate* accuracy (and to supply the `cost_gt` IC target). Your extension conditions the encoder on the IC/BC while keeping these loss terms and their equal weights unchanged for whatever specification is being trained.

4. **Benchmarks (PDE families) and the initial-condition zoo.** A 1D convection–diffusion–reaction (CDR) family `u_t + β u_x − ν u_xx − ρ u(1−u) = 0` on a periodic domain (Fisher reaction `ρu(1−u)`), specialized to convection (β), diffusion (ν), reaction (ρ), and combinations; and a 2D Helmholtz family with analytic solution. The reference initial conditions are enumerated in `data_gen/systems.py::function(u0)` — a rich zoo including amplitude-scaled sines (`0.1sin(x)`, `0.5sin(x)`, `sin(x)`, `10sin(x)`, `50sin(x)`), offset sines (`1+sin(x)`, `2+sin(x)`, `6+sin(x)`, `10+sin(x)`), frequencies (`sin(x)`, `sin(2x)`), Gaussians of two widths (`gauss` σ=π/4, `gauss_pi_2` σ=π/2), and other shapes (`tanh(x)`, `2x`, `x^2`, `sin^2(x)`, `sin(x)cos(x)`). **This zoo is your initial-condition space**: it is naturally parameterized (an amplitude, an offset, a frequency, a Gaussian width/center) and it contains distinct *functional families* (sinusoids vs. Gaussians vs. polynomials) — so "unseen IC parameter" and "unseen IC function family" are both well-defined and buildable from the reference construction unchanged. The reference solvers are **periodic** (spectral/FFT); varying the boundary condition to Dirichlet/Neumann/mixed requires a reference solver you author and validate (see the stretch objective and the reference-validity rules).

5. **Metrics.** Relative L2 error `‖û − u‖_2 / ‖u‖_2` and absolute L2 error, averaged over the evaluated specifications, reported as a mean over multiple seeds. Reference solutions follow the reference implementation: reaction and convection (and diffusion) have closed-form solutions (reaction the logistic solution; convection/diffusion the exact spectral solution), and reaction-diffusion uses operator splitting (reaction then diffusion) — build the reference for each family accordingly rather than defaulting to a numerical solver, and, for any non-periodic boundary condition you add, author and validate the reference (rules below).

6. **Reference implementation.** The authors' reference code is provided at `code/P2INN/` (`model.py` = the parameter/coordinate encoders and the concatenation-and-decode; `Loss_f.py` = the PDE-residual loss; `config.py`, `dataloader.py`, `train.py`; `data_gen/` = the on-the-fly PDE/reference-solution generation, including `systems.py::function`). This defines the architecture, the training budget, and the evaluation you hold fixed; inspect and reuse it (do not reimplement the model and present that as the contribution) and add your specification encoder and its analysis alongside it. (The repository ships **no license file**; treat it as a read-only reference to hold the held-fixed components fixed, and cite the paper.)

### Anti-cheating

The contract is about honest, attributable generalization from structured conditioning. The following are not allowed:

- **Training on the held-out specifications.** The model trains only on the trained specifications; every held-out set (unseen IC parameters, unseen IC families, unseen BCs, unseen combinations, held-out PDE compositions) is evaluated, never trained on, and never used to select any hyperparameter.
- **Tuning on the held-out sets.** Encoder/representation hyperparameters and the conditioning design are selected on a seen-specification validation split, not on any held-out generalization error.
- **Scientific protocol compliance and truthful disclosure.** Architectural changes beyond the specification encoder are not permitted, even if disclosed, ablated, and compared under matched computational budgets. The base-loss terms and weights, collocation, optimizer, epochs, and seeds remain held fixed across the headline comparison arms. The optional under-training diagnostic retains its explicitly permitted budget increases. A disclosed prohibited change fails the scientific protocol requirement; an affirmative false claim that the executed implementation obeys a held-fixed constraint is assessed separately as an integrity violation. A scientific shortfall alone is not evidence of deception.
- **A naive-input win in disguise.** The structured encoding must beat the matched-capacity naive control (IC/BC as raw extra inputs). A "win" that only reflects the extra input capacity — one the naive control matches — is not a contribution.
- **A degenerate or ignored encoding.** The IC/BC input may not be ignored (the ablation must actually degrade held-out accuracy) nor collapsed to a constant; the model must genuinely condition on the specification.
- **A fabricated or unvalidated reference.** For any non-periodic BC or new PDE composition, the reference solution comes from a solver you author and **validate** (convergence + agreement with the spectral reference on the periodic overlap); measuring "generalization" against an unvalidated or hand-set reference is fabrication.
- **Overclaiming a generalization boundary.** Boundary-mapping is optional; but *if* you claim a **fundamental** generalization boundary, it must be a **meaningful** specification (not an adversarial/degenerate strawman), shown to persist under a **substantial (~2×–4×)** increase in the parameter-encoder-path capacity, epochs, and collocation and against a **converged reference** (a token bump does not count), and **method-specific** — on a non-linear-IC family, or with the matched naive control **not** sharing the failure. - **A trivially-solvable IC axis.** The headline core IC generalization may not rest on a family where the solution is a single linear operator on the initial condition (pure convection/diffusion amplitude scaling), where the naive control generalizes for free; at least one core held-out IC axis must be on a non-linear-IC family (reaction, reaction-diffusion, or CDR).
- **A rigged or cherry-picked split.** The trained/held-out splits are fixed before results; you may not choose them after the fact to flatter the method, nor report only the specifications where it wins.
- **Benchmark tampering.** The existing PDE definitions, domains, periodic reference construction, and metrics are unaltered.
