# Task: Develop a Substantive Follow-up to "Efficient Berry Phase Calculation via Adaptive Variational Quantum Computing Approach"

## Objective — Here's what you're doing

Develop a substantive scientific follow-up to the AVQDS-based Berry-phase algorithm by Mootz and Yao, "Efficient Berry phase calculation via adaptive variational quantum computing approach," 2025. Your contribution is to **characterize the resilience of this algorithm to realistic quantum hardware noise — establishing, with rigorous evidence, the noise levels below which the computed Berry phase stays accurate, and explaining why the method behaves the way it does under noise.** The original paper reports only noiseless state-vector results; your follow-up asks the question the paper leaves open — *how much noise can this method tolerate before its topological output degrades* — and answers it as a self-contained academic paper.

## The Problem — Here's why it matters

The **Berry phase** is a geometric property of quantum states that underlies the quantum Hall effect, topological insulators, and topological superconductors; for a time-reversal-invariant 1D crystalline system it is quantized, taking the value $0$ in a topologically trivial phase and $\pi$ in a topologically nontrivial phase. Computing it for interacting many-body systems on a classical computer is hard, which motivates quantum algorithms that evolve the system under a cyclic Hamiltonian and read the accumulated phase off a quantum circuit.

The original paper's advance is efficiency: by combining adaptive variational quantum dynamics simulation (AVQDS) with a time-reversed adiabatic loop, it computes the Berry phase in circuits of a few tens to a few hundred layers — orders of magnitude shallower than a Trotterized adiabatic evolution, which for the same 4-site chain would need on the order of $10^5$–$10^6$ CNOTs ($\gtrsim 10^3\times$ more). Shallow circuits are what make the method a candidate for near-term hardware.

But the paper's results are **entirely noiseless**: all Berry phases are obtained from exact state-vector simulation, "neglecting errors from quantum gates and finite sampling." The scientific question this leaves open is the one that decides whether the shallow-circuit advantage actually matters in practice: **under realistic device noise, how accurate does the Berry-phase estimate remain, and at what noise level does it break?** Two facts make this non-trivial rather than a routine sweep. First, the Berry phase is read out from a **measured probability**, so **finite-shot sampling noise is intrinsic and irreducible** — it persists even for a fault-tolerant device, because the final logical measurement is still a projective sample. Second, the method's noiseless robustness (it returns the correct phase even under strong non-adiabaticity) comes from two phase contributions that **co-evolve along the same variational trajectory so their non-adiabatic errors cancel** ($\varphi_B=\varphi_{\text{qc}}+\varphi_G$). That cancellation is a property of the co-evolving, adaptively grown ansatz — and it does not automatically carry over to *stochastic* device noise. In the required scope of this task (below) the ansatz and the variational trajectory $\theta(t)$ are frozen at their noiseless values (so the McLachlan integration is not re-run under noise), but the quantities the device must measure to evaluate $\varphi_G$ — the energy expectation $\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}\rangle$ that feeds $\varphi_{G,1}$ and the geometric-connection overlaps $\langle\Psi|\partial_{\theta_\mu}\Psi\rangle$ that feed $\varphi_{G,2}$ — are re-evaluated under the same noise as the readout, so **both $\varphi_{\text{qc}}$ and $\varphi_G$ carry stochastic noise** and the extracted threshold is a genuine whole-method number. The natural question is then how the two contributions' errors combine — the *deterministic* bias they share through the frozen trajectory can partially cancel, but their *independent* stochastic (shot/incoherent) fluctuations add rather than cancel, and $\varphi_{G,1}=-\int\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}\rangle\,dt'$ is a dynamical-phase leg whose measured-$\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}\rangle$ noise integrates over the loop into a non-cancelling drift distinct from the $\varphi_{\text{qc}}$/$\varphi_{G,2}$ overlaps; feeding the sampling noise all the way back into the McLachlan integration (noisy $M,V$, so the trajectory itself becomes stochastic) is the harder, optional study.

## Background — Here's what you need to know about the original paper

Read `paper.md` in full before beginning. The algorithm you will characterize (and hold fixed) has these components:

1. **Cyclic adiabatic evolution.** A Hamiltonian $\hat{\mathcal{H}}(\lambda)$ depends on a cyclic parameter $\lambda(t)\in[0,2\pi]$ with $\hat{\mathcal{H}}(2\pi)=\hat{\mathcal{H}}(0)$. The ground state adiabatically follows $|G(\lambda(t))\rangle$, accumulating $|G(2\pi)\rangle = e^{-i\varphi_D}e^{i\varphi_B}|G(0)\rangle$ with dynamical phase $\varphi_D$ and Berry phase $\varphi_B = -i\oint d\lambda\,\langle G(\lambda)|\partial_\lambda G(\lambda)\rangle$.

2. **Time-reversal-symmetric loop** (Eq. 13): $U_{\text{loop}} = U_\circlearrowleft(0,T/2)\,U_\circlearrowleft(T/2,0)$ — forward evolution $\lambda:0\to\pi$ then time-reversed propagation $\lambda:\pi\to 2\pi$. For a time-reversal-invariant Hamiltonian this cancels $\varphi_D$ exactly while $\varphi_B$ adds.

3. **AVQITE ground-state preparation.** An adaptive ansatz $|\Psi[\theta]\rangle=\prod_\mu e^{-i\theta_\mu\hat{\mathcal{A}}_\mu}|\Psi_0\rangle$ is grown by appending pool operators to keep the McLachlan distance $L^2$ below a cutoff. Used to prepare $|G(\lambda=0)\rangle$.

4. **AVQDS adaptive time evolution.** The same adaptive structure applied to real-time cyclic dynamics: variational parameters evolve via the McLachlan equation of motion $\sum_\nu M_{\mu\nu}\dot\theta_\nu = V_\mu$ (Eq. 6), integrated with RK4 at adaptive step size ($\delta\theta_{\max}=0.01$); when $L^2\ge L^2_{\text{cut}}$ the ansatz is expanded (in the disjoint-support "TETRIS" form that keeps circuits shallow).

5. **Berry-phase readout (Hadamard test, Fig. 1).** An ancilla measures the overlap phase: the ancilla's $|0\rangle$ probability is $P_{|0\rangle}=\tfrac12(1+\cos\varphi_{\text{qc}})$, from which $\varphi_{\text{qc}}$ is inferred. Because AVQDS does not track the global phase, a separately integrated variational phase $\varphi_G$ (Eq. 5) is added: $\varphi_B = \varphi_{\text{qc}} + \varphi_G$. **The final Berry phase is the sum of two separately obtained quantities** — one from a measured probability ($\varphi_{\text{qc}}$), one from a classical-side integration (Eq. 5) of device-measured quantities along the trajectory: the energy expectation $\langle\hat{\mathcal{H}}\rangle$ (giving $\varphi_{G,1}$) and the geometric-connection overlaps $\langle\Psi|\partial_{\theta_\mu}\Psi\rangle$ (giving $\varphi_{G,2}$). ($M,V$ enter only the McLachlan equation of motion, Eq. 6, not $\varphi_G$.)

6. **Hyperparameters.** $L^2_{\text{cut}}=10^{-4}$ for AVQDS, $\delta\theta_{\max}=0.01$ for the adaptive RK4 step, and the UCCSD-like operator pool (products of Paulis with an odd number of $\hat\sigma^y$); for the AVQDS evolution the pool is the set of Hamiltonian Pauli strings ($N_H=18$ for $U=0$, $N_H=22$ for $U=10$).

7. **SSHH test bed** (Eq. 19). A dimerized Fermi-Hubbard (Su–Schrieffer–Heeger–Hubbard) chain, staggered hopping $t_{j,j+1}=t(1+(-1)^j\delta)$, on-site $U$, particle-hole symmetric. $N=4$ sites, $t=1$, mapped to **8 qubits** by Jordan–Wigner; the twist $\lambda$ is applied to spin-up fermions only. Two interaction regimes: $U=0$ (noninteracting SSH) and $U=10$ (strongly correlated). The topological character is set by the dimerization $\delta$, with a phase transition at $\delta=0$.

**Reference baseline behavior (noiseless, from the paper):**

- **Berry phase.** Reproduces the exact-diagonalization value to $\sim 10^{-3}$ or better in both regimes: $\varphi_B=0$ in the topologically trivial regime, $\varphi_B=\pi$ in the nontrivial regime. All loop times $T$ resolve the $\delta=0$ transition.
- **Circuit resources.** Peak circuit depth up to **106 layers** ($U=0$) and **279 layers** ($U=10$); the nontrivial region costs several times more CNOTs than the trivial region. The AVQITE ground-state preparation adds $\sim 20$ CNOTs ($U=0$) / $\sim 92$ CNOTs ($U=10$).
- **Trotter comparison ($U=0$).** A first-order Trotterized adiabatic evolution at matched accuracy needs $\sim 10^3\times$ more CNOTs (e.g. $\sim 4.8\times 10^5$ at $T=20$), which is the sense in which AVQDS is "shallow."
- **Noiseless robustness the paper emphasizes.** The time-reversed loop cancels the dynamical phase; the method returns the correct $\varphi_B$ even for short loops with strong non-adiabaticity, because $\varphi_B$ is a gauge-invariant closed-loop quantity and the fast dynamical phases of excited-state admixtures average out over the loop. **This robustness is to non-adiabaticity, established under exact arithmetic — it is not a claim about stochastic hardware noise, and the two are physically different.**

**Reference data.** `data/figshare_data/AVQS-Berry.zip` contains the authors' per-$\lambda$ traces: `results{run}p0.h5` (AVQDS phase-component, fidelity, per-layer CNOT and depth traces) and `exact{run}p0.h5` (exact-diagonalization reference). These are the ground truth for reproducing the noiseless numbers and validating any re-implementation. Note the phase traces are stored **unfolded / wrapped**, not as a clean $0$-or-$\pi$ column: the reported Berry phase is reconstructed from the stored per-$\lambda$ phase components and folded to the principal value (the authors' figure scripts evaluate it at the loop endpoint and take the magnitude, yielding $0$ in the trivial and $\pi$ in the nontrivial regime). Reproduction against the archive must be judged **after** you apply and state a branch/fold/sign convention; do not read a trace column directly as the Berry phase. The archive does **not** contain the AVQITE/AVQDS engine (the authors' driver only invokes an external code base). It does ship the authors' figure-generation scripts, and — for the $U=10$ runs — per-run `incar` files holding the Jordan–Wigner-mapped Hamiltonian and operator pools, plus per-run `ansatz.h5` files holding an already-grown, already-optimized ansatz (operator selections and parameters). You must implement the engine, construct the Hamiltonian and Jordan–Wigner mapping yourself, and grow your own ansatz: these shipped artifacts may be **inspected and cross-checked against, but not loaded in place of your own construction** (see the held-fixed and reward-hacking rules).
