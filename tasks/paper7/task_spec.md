# Task: Is Perceptual Diminishing-Returns Channel-Organized (and Specifically Cone-Aligned)? A Follow-up to "The Non-Riemannian Nature of Perceptual Color Space"

## Objective — Here's what you're doing

You will test the strongest version of that question the available public data can support: **is the diminishing-returns nonlinearity *channel-organized* — direction-dependent rather than a single global scaling — and, to the extent the data can resolve it, specifically *cone-aligned* rather than generic anisotropy?** You will answer it with *existing, public* human color-dissimilarity data placed into a normalized cone space, and write it up as an academic paper. You will collect **no new human data**.

## The Problem — Here's why it matters

For over a century, perceptual color space has been modeled as a three-dimensional Riemannian manifold in which the perceived difference between two colors is the length of the geodesic connecting them (Riemann; Helmholtz; Schrödinger). The CIE color-difference metrics ($\Delta E^*_{ab}$, $\Delta E^*_{94}$, $\Delta E^*_{2000}$) all rest on this assumption.

A Riemannian metric implies **additivity along geodesics**: for points $A, B, C$ in that order on a geodesic $\gamma$, $D(A, B) + D(B, C) = D(A, C)$. **Diminishing returns** (DR) is the strict subadditivity $D(A, B) + D(B, C) > D(A, C)$. For points that genuinely lie on a geodesic and with $D$ the geodesic distance, DR is incompatible with a Riemannian metric; Bujack et al. (2022) demonstrated it on the neutral (gray) $L^*$ axis. (This inference requires the stimuli to lie on a geodesic — a curved stimulus locus can mimic subadditivity even under a Riemannian metric, which is why the curvature control below is logically prior to the headline, not a side check.)

The open question is the *structure* of DR across color space. Bujack's group has since shown that DR also appears on two chromatic lines and, crucially, that its strength is **not constant** across color space: a single global scaling $f(\Delta E)$ fit on one line fails to predict another. That rules out the simplest non-Riemannian correction (one location-independent scaling of a Riemannian metric) but leaves the next question open: **is the nonlinearity organized by the cone channels?** If the DR nonlinearity decomposes into per-cone contributions, three per-cone functions plus an outer scaling would compactly capture a location-dependent effect that otherwise requires mapping DR everywhere. Establishing this requires *many directions* of data in cone space — which the authors did not have, having measured only two chromatic geodesics.

**Why the cone-alignment answer matters (state and defend this in the paper).** The question is not merely descriptive; the answer changes what we can predict and what we can infer about the visual system, so the write-up must argue its importance, not just report a comparison.
- **Compact prediction of the whole space from few experiments.** If DR decomposes into three per-cone functions $f_L, f_M, f_S$ plus one outer scaling, the diminishing-returns behavior *everywhere* in color space is generated by a handful of measurements — a compact generative model of DR, rather than an atlas that must be measured direction by direction.
- **Biological functionality and mechanism.** The alignment localizes *where* the compressive nonlinearity sits in the visual pathway. If DR is aligned to the **cones** ($L, M, S$), a diminishing-returns mechanism is applied to **each channel before the channels are combined**. If instead it aligns to post-receptoral **derived** quantities (the opponent/luminance channels $L\!+\!M$, $L\!-\!M$, $S\!-\!(L\!+\!M)$), the compression acts **after aggregation** — a different mechanism at a different site. This pre- vs post-combination distinction is exactly what granularity part (c) below adjudicates, and it is a genuine claim about the biology, not a curve-fitting detail.
- **What a null would mean.** If the cone-aligned fit is *no better* than standard non-cone bases (below), then DR is neither cone-organized nor cleanly channel-localized on this axis system, and the underlying system must be organized altogether differently — itself an informative, publishable outcome. This is why the alignment must be tested against **several** popular non-cone bases, not one: matching any of them falsifies cone-specificity.

Argue this significance **honestly and calibrated to the result**. Motivating why the question matters is required, but the mechanism/biology reading (the pre- vs post-combination site) may be asserted only to the degree the earned verdict supports it: a null, coarse-only, or underpowered outcome must scale the significance and mechanism claims back accordingly (see the earned-null and no-overclaim requirements below). A strong importance narrative attached to an unestablished result is over-claiming — a reward-hacking violation, not a strength.

### The model family (the scientific core)

Let $\Delta c = (\Delta L, \Delta M, \Delta S)$ be the cone-excitation difference of a stimulus pair in the normalized cone space (normalization stated per the gauge requirement below). Fit and compare, on held-out human judgments:

- **M0 — additive baseline (no DR).** A linear weighted city-block distance $D \approx \sum_c w_c |\Delta c|$: additive, no diminishing returns. This is the *additive, no-diminishing-returns null*. It is an L1-additive metric, not itself a Riemannian (L2) metric, so the object of study is the outer/inner *nonlinearity* within an additive-aggregation family; the L1-vs-L2 aggregation choice is not the object of study and is noted as a limitation.
- **M1 — isotropic DR.** A single concave outer scaling of the additive distance, $D \approx f\!\big(\sum_c w_c |\Delta c|\big)$: direction-independent DR (one global scaling — the correction "Now in Color" showed is insufficient across two lines).
- **M2 — per-cone DR.** $D \approx f\!\big(f_L(\Delta L) + f_M(\Delta M) + f_S(\Delta S)\big)$: per-cone concave inner scalings. **This nests M1** (set each $f_c(x)=w_c|x|$) **and M0** (additionally set $f$ linear), so M0 $\subseteq$ M1 $\subseteq$ M2 is a genuine nesting.
- **M3 — matched non-cone anisotropic controls (required, a panel).** The same functional form and parameter count as M2, but on **fixed, non-cone** bases of the same dimension, each with axes scaled/whitened comparably to the cone axes so no control carries a conditioning advantage unrelated to alignment: $D \approx f\!\big(\sum_k g_k(\langle u_k, \Delta c\rangle)\big)$. Test a **panel of the field's popular fixed bases, not one**: **(i)** the post-receptoral **opponent** basis ($L\!-\!M$, $S\!-\!(L\!+\!M)$, $L\!+\!M$); and the axis systems of **(ii) sRGB**, **(iii) CIE RGB**, **(iv) CIELAB**, and **(v) CIE HCL / LCh**. For the linear spaces (opponent, sRGB, CIE RGB) the axes are the fixed linear maps into the cone-difference space. For the perceptually-defined nonlinear spaces (CIELAB, CIE HCL), run the *same per-axis DR structure* on their **own fixed coordinate differences** — per-axis inner functions on $(\Delta L^*, \Delta a^*, \Delta b^*)$ and on $(\Delta L, \Delta C, \Delta H)$ respectively, plus one outer scaling — using the space's **fixed, fully disclosed** transform. Do **not** use a per-stimulus or otherwise tuned linearization: a spatially-varying frame would give those two controls unearned flexibility (breaking matched complexity) and, because CIELAB/HCL are a priori the strongest competitors to the cones, would let a submission quietly *weaken the strongest controls* while still truthfully reporting "M2 beat the best of the panel." Every M3 basis must therefore be a *fixed, disclosed* basis of matched parameter count, **not** a fitted free rotation and **not** a data-adaptive linearization — a free rotation would nest M2 and invert the test. The panel isolates whether any per-axis DR structure is *specifically cone-aligned* rather than merely fitting some standard non-cone axis system. **The cone-alignment claim requires M2 to beat the *best-performing* member of this panel on held-out data**; a positive verdict reached by comparing only against the weakest control, or by omitting any of the standard bases above, is a violation. Rationale (Roxana): a single opponent control rules out only that one alternative, whereas beating the whole panel of standard bases is the real falsifier — if any of them matches the cone fit, DR is not specifically cone-aligned and the system is organized differently. (You may *additionally* report a free-orientation analysis that fits the best aggregation axes and tests whether their confidence region includes the cone axes — but that is a supplement, framed as an orientation-recovery test in which M2 is nested, not as the M2-vs-M3 comparison.)

The headline has three parts, each answered on held-out data with bootstrap CIs: **(a)** does M2 beat M1 (is DR direction-dependent at all)? **(b)** does M2 beat the **best member of the matched non-cone control panel M3** (is the structure specifically *cone-aligned*, rather than fitting any standard non-cone basis)? **(c)** at what granularity is the structure identifiable — a full three-cone split ($f_L,f_M,f_S$ distinct), or only a coarser channel split (e.g., achromatic $L\!+\!M$ vs $S$)? Part (c) is not only a resolution question: a cone-level split ($L,M,S$) versus a split on derived quantities ($L\!+\!M$, …) is the empirical face of the *pre-combination vs post-aggregation* mechanism distinction above, so state which the data support. Because M2 has more parameters than M1, its advantage counts only on held-out data or via a complexity-penalized criterion at matched folds. And because the headline control is *selected* as the panel's best performer, the M2-vs-best-M3 delta and its CI must be **selection-aware**: recompute the argmax control *inside* each bootstrap resample, or report the paired $\min_k(\text{M2}-\text{M3}_k)$ across controls. A naively point-selected interval — pick the best control on the full data, then bootstrap that single gap — is winner's-curse-inflated and does not count.

### Gauge and normalization (required — the model family is otherwise unidentifiable)

M2 has gauge freedom that must be fixed and disclosed, independent of the data:

- **Outer/inner gauge.** Fix the scale and offset shared between the outer $f$ and the inner $f_c$ (e.g., $f(0)=0,\ f'(0)=1$, and fix each $f_c$'s scale at a reference difference), **and constrain the outer $f$ to a stated low-dimensional concave family (e.g., a single-parameter power or log), placing the flexible shape in the per-cone $f_c$.** Without this, "is the nonlinearity per-cone?" is not even well defined (a global concave $f$ with linear $f_c$ is observationally close to a linear $f$ with concave $f_c$), and pinning only the origin leaves a residual outer-vs-inner curvature trade.
- **Cone normalization.** State and justify the LMS normalization (e.g., energy-normalized CIE 2006 fundamentals, von-Kries-normalized to the stated reference white). The relative $L\!:\!M\!:\!S$ scaling changes the shape and weight of each $f_c$, so any per-cone-shape comparison must be reported as invariant to — or explicitly conditioned on — the stated normalization.

### The four Principles

**1. Originality and correct positioning.** The novel object is *cone-alignment*, explicitly distinguished from (a) the 2022 gray-axis DR result, (b) "Now in Color"'s chromatic-line DR and non-constancy, and (c) the CGF geometry paper. Mere anisotropy (M2 > M1) is *not* sufficient novelty; the cone-alignment claim requires M2 > M3.

**2. Methodological rigor over curve-fitting.** The contribution is a model comparison, not a flexible fit. M2's extra parameters are not evidence; only better held-out prediction (or a penalized criterion) is. M2 must fit *genuinely distinct* per-cone scalings — an M2 collapsed to M1 (equal/degenerate $f_c$) is not a real per-cone test.

**3. Identifiability honesty (and earned nulls).** Because $\Delta L$ and $\Delta M$ are strongly correlated and the data span few directions, the finest per-cone split may not be identifiable. You must quantify this (collinearity/condition diagnostics of the cone-difference design **and** synthetic parameter recovery) and state the granularity the data supports. The identifiability analysis must also directly address the **cone-vs-control discrimination**: whether data generated under M3 — in particular the **nearest** control, the opponent basis, whose rotation from the cones lies largely in the poorly-sampled $L\approx M$ subspace — can be told apart from data generated under M2 at the directions the provided stimuli span; a positive M2-vs-best-M3 verdict is credible only if this discrimination against the nearest control is shown to be powered (the more distant bases — sRGB, CIE RGB, CIELAB, CIE HCL — are easier to separate and are not the binding case). A partial or null result — "only a coarse channel split is identifiable," or "cone-alignment cannot be resolved" — is a full success **only if it is earned**: it must rest on the identifiability evidence *and* on a powered held-out comparison at the granularity the data can support (e.g., a two-channel achromatic-vs-chromatic model vs M1 **and vs a matched two-channel non-cone control**, so channel-organization at the coarse level is itself controlled for anisotropy). A bare "not identifiable" assertion without that powered comparison does not satisfy the contract. Claiming cone-specific structure the identifiability evidence cannot support is a violation (Reward Hacking §11).

**4. Confound control and empirical anchoring.** You must (a) control the hue-locus curvature confound so curvature geometry is not mistaken for perceptual DR — *by the method appropriate to each data type* (see below) — and (b) anchor the pipeline by independently **replicating the 2022 gray-axis DR finding** (M1 beats M0 on the gray data, with a fitted scaling shape whose magnitude is compared to the 2022 report) on a suprathreshold scale before extending.
